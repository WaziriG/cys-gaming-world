1What Has Been Built

You now own a real website called Nano Games — think of it like your very own game store on the internet, except every game in it can be one YOU made up.

Right now your store has 2 games in it, and 2 empty shelves ("Coming Soon" cards) waiting for whatever you dream up next.

🐢 Game 1 — Aquatic Survival

You play as Aqua, a tough little turtle, fighting off wave after wave of enemies and giant animal bosses underwater and on land. The goal: survive as long as you can.

⚔️ Game 2 — Elemental Trials

You battle 7 elemental guardians (Water, Fire, Earth, Lightning, Ice, Poison, Wind), and there's even a secret hidden world with 5 MORE bosses if you find the secret portal. You collect weapons, merge them together to make them stronger, and can even loop the whole game again on higher difficulty for better rewards.

🏆 The Trophy Case (Leaderboard)

Both games have a "Top Scores" button. When someone finishes a run, they can type their name in and their score gets saved forever — so you (and everyone who plays) can see who's the best in the world at your games.

Fun fact: every game has its OWN trophy case. Aquatic Survival scores don't mix with Elemental Trials scores — each game gets to crown its own champion.

You don't need to know how to code to be the CEO — but every good boss understands roughly how their company runs. Here's the simple version:

Think of it like a real arcade building: the website is the storefront, each game is an arcade cabinet, the trophy case is the high-score board bolted to the wall, and Vercel is the electric company making sure the lights never go out.

3Your Job: Game Designer & CEO

As the CEO, YOU decide what games go into your arcade. Here's how a new game gets from "idea in your head" to "playable on the website":

  1. Dream it up. What's the game about? Who's the hero? What are the bad guys? How do you win?
  2. Build or describe it. You can build the whole game yourself, or describe it in detail and work with a grown-up (and Claude) to build it.
  3. Hand off the finished file. One game = one file. When it's ready to play, it gets handed over to be added to the arcade.
  4. It gets wired in. The trophy case gets connected, a card gets made for the home page, and it goes live for the whole world to play.
You are the Game Designer. That means the ideas, the characters, the difficulty, the art style, the vibe — all of that is 100% your call. Nobody can tell you your game idea is wrong.

Things a good Game Designer does

  • Plays their OWN games a lot, to find what's fun and what's boring.
  • Writes down ideas the second they think of them (keep a notebook!).
  • Isn't afraid to change something that isn't fun yet.
  • Decides which "Coming Soon" shelf gets filled next.

4Telling Friends & Family

A store with no customers isn't much of a business. Here's how to get people to visit (with a grown-up's help sending links):

Your elevator pitch

"I built a video game website called Nano Games. It's got a turtle survival game and a boss-battle game with 12 bosses. You can play for free right in your browser — no downloading anything. Try to beat my high score!"

Easy ways to spread the word

  • Send the link in a family group chat: "Check out the arcade I made!"
  • Challenge them — "Bet you can't beat my score of ___." A challenge always gets people to click.
  • Show, don't just tell — play it in front of them for 30 seconds so they see how fun it is.
  • Ask them to share it with 2 more people. That's how something small grows big.
  • Update people when a new game goes live — "New game just dropped!" is exciting news.
