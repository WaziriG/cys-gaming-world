2. The Mystery of the Twin Files 👯

There were two copies of Nano Slime on the computer. They looked the same. I compared them line by line, and the game part was identical — same monsters, same pumpkins, same slime forms.

But one had a hidden problem. It could not save.

Think of two backpacks

They look the same. You put your homework in one and carry it to school — it's still there. You put your homework in the other, and by the time you get to school it's gone, because that backpack has a hole in the bottom.

Play the broken Nano Slime and close the tab, and everything you earned disappears. All your goo. Your costumes. Your best form.

The dangerous part: the broken one looks completely normal. No error message. No warning. It just quietly forgets. I put the good copy on your site, so progress now sticks.

I also deleted an old leftover copy of Aqua Survivor that your site wasn't using. Nothing was lost — your project uses Git, which remembers every version of every file forever, like a time machine.

New word: a "bug" — a mistake in code that makes a program do the wrong thing. The worst bugs are the quiet ones that don't look like mistakes at all.

3. The Big One: Leaderboards 🏆

A leaderboard is the list of top scores. Before today, only 3 of your games had one: Aqua Survivor, Elemental Trials, and Haunted Mansion.

Here's the problem. The instructions for building that leaderboard were written out three separate times — about 390 lines of code, copied into each game.

Imagine writing your game rules in three notebooks

Then you find a mistake. Now you have to fix it in all three. Forget one, and that notebook has different rules than the others — and nobody knows which is right.

I rewrote it so there's one single copy that all the games share. One rulebook everybody reads from.

1,551 lines → 40 lines

Same features. Way less code.

Now a fix in one place reaches every game at once. And giving a brand new game a leaderboard takes about 6 lines instead of 390.

New word: "don't repeat yourself" — a rule real programmers follow. If you're copying and pasting the same code over and over, write it once and share it instead.

4. Decisions I Made (And Why) 🤔

Sprite Forge gets NO leaderboard

Sprite Forge is a drawing tool, not a game. No way to win, no way to lose, so no score. A board with nothing to rank would just confuse people. 7 games have leaderboards. Sprite Forge doesn't.

Anthill is ranked by TIER, not FOOD

This is the one I really want you to get.

Anthill is an idle game — your ants keep collecting food even while the game is closed, up to 8 hours' worth. So imagine the board ranked players by total food. Who wins?

Not the best player — the most patient one

A kid who plays really well for an hour might collect 5,000 food. A kid who taps once, closes the tab, goes to school, and comes back 8 hours later might have more — having done nothing.

The board wouldn't measure skill. It'd measure who waited longest.

So Anthill ranks by colony tier — how far you actually got. Food still shows, but only to break ties. Waiting helps a little; the top spot goes to whoever played best.

6. The Games Can Talk Now 🔊

For most of today those four boards were empty. The scoreboard was built and hanging on the wall, but the games didn't shout out the score when you finished. The board was listening. Nobody was talking.

Now they talk. Three were easy — Luffy's Quest and Quadratic Quest already had a clear "game over" moment, and Nano Slime has the moment your slime dies. Each one now calls out its score, and the board hears it.

Anthill was the interesting one. It has no ending at all — the colony just keeps growing. So there's no moment to shout at.

So we flipped it around

Every other game: the game tells the scoreboard when you finish.

Anthill: the scoreboard asks the game. When you press SUBMIT, the board taps the game on the shoulder and says "how's the colony doing?" — and the game answers.

Same scoreboard. Opposite direction. When a problem doesn't fit, sometimes you turn it around instead of forcing it.

One more detail: Nano Slime's sandbox is for testing builds, so scores from it do not go to the board. That'd be like practicing with cheat codes on and then putting it on the record.

I checked that the code is valid and that real scores are accepted. But nobody has actually played them yet — that part is your job.

7. One Last Thing: Cheating 😈

Your games run in the web browser, so someone who knows how could fake a score. We can't fully stop that. But I set a maximum for every game — if Anthill's highest tier is 50, the site refuses anything above 50. A cheater can't post 999,999,999. The board stays believable.
